About

I am a third-year B.Sc. student in mathematics and computer science at the Université de Montréal, doing undergraduate research in machine learning in the Department of Mathematics & Statistics. My interests center on AI safety and alignment (model organisms, evaluations, agentic misalignment), world models, and machine learning for sequential decision-making — with side threads in robotics / embodied ML and remote sensing.

Publications

  • RFI Detection in Sentinel-1 SAR Imagery via Pseudo-Label Distillation and Multi-Resolution Ensemble

    A. Elghoudane. IEEE International Conference on Image Processing (ICIP), 2026 — abstract accepted, sole author. ESA ClearSAR Track 1 Grand Challenge (AI4QC, satellite-data quality control).

    Radio-frequency interference corrupts large swaths of Sentinel-1 synthetic-aperture radar imagery, and hand-labelling it at mission scale is infeasible. The method pairs pseudo-label distillation — bootstrapping supervision from an ensemble teacher instead of manual annotation — with a multi-resolution ensemble that reconciles detections across scales, giving a detector that trains without exhaustive RFI labels and stays robust on genuinely unseen scenes.

Research & projects

  • Neurodiversity-Aware Composite Guardrail for Youth Mental-Health AI SafetyFlagship

    Mila × Bell × Buzz HPC × Kids Help Phone AI Safety Hackathon — Team Lead (5); Special Mention, top 7 of 80+ teams; $250 team prize.

    An input guardrail for a youth crisis-support chatbot: a Drift Diffusion Model (turn-by-turn risk accumulation, parameters tuned for neurodivergent communication patterns) fused with a bilingual (EN/FR) multi-model LLM-as-judge ensemble, whose design I led. F1 0.850 (precision 0.871, recall 0.831) on the official test set; 61.6% pass on a 458-case DEI-stratified adversarial stress suite (+8.6 pp over the prior version).

    Code lives in the hackathon organizers' private GitHub org (it touches sensitive youth crisis-support data under the event's data-handling terms).

  • Foresight-Induced Instrumental Harm in LLM Agents

    AI-safety “model organisms” research — working paper (in preparation).

    Sugarscape-style multi-agent survival simulations testing whether giving an LLM agent the predicted consequences of its candidate actions — a world-model / lookahead signal, in the spirit of Yann LeCun's critique that today's LLMs are purely reactive — changes its willingness to take a harmful-but-instrumentally-optimal action. Preliminary evidence: the capability is emergent with model scale, and the induced harm is driven specifically by the consequence information itself.

    Repository (private during review — available on request)
